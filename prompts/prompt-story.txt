PROMPT 2: READ-ALOUD STORY BUILT FROM A VOCABULARY FILE
Copy everything between the two lines of dashes into a new Claude chat.
Fill in the [BRACKETS] and attach or paste your vocabulary file.
=====================================================================
----------------------------------------------------------------------
I'm preparing for [the Ausbildung zur Medizinischen
Technologin für Laboratoriumsanalytik]. Attached is my vocabulary file
(tab-separated: german, english, phrase). Write a German story that uses
these words, so I can listen to it as an audio book while walking. A local
app reads the text aloud with a German voice.

WHAT THE STORY SHOULD BE

- Length: about [60] minutes of audio. Measured on a
  previous story, 10,000 words came out as 92 minutes including all
  pauses, so allow about 110 words per minute: 60 minutes is about 6,500
  words, 90 minutes about 10,000. Stay within 10% of the target.
- Use every word from the german column at least once, in exactly that
  form. For nouns, the part after der/die/das must appear as written (see
  the rules below). Where it fits naturally, reuse the example sentences
  from the phrase column word for word. Then the audio for those sentences
  matches the vocabulary track I've already practised.
- A real story with characters and a thread, not a list of definitions.
  For example, a trainee in their first year, alternating between school
  and the workplace. Each chapter covers one topic from the vocabulary, and
  the ideas are explained in dialogue, in the order they build on each
  other.
- German at B1 level: short, clear sentences, mostly 8 to 18 words. Use
  the present tense wherever possible.

HOW THE APP READS THE TEXT (this decides the format)

- Plain UTF-8 text, no markdown: no *, bullet points or numbered lists.
  The only special character is # at the start of a chapter heading line
  (see below).
- An empty line starts a new paragraph, which gets a 1.5 s pause. Every
  sentence gets a 0.6 s pause after it. A line break without an empty
  line is ignored.
- Sentences are split at . ! ? or … followed by a space. So every
  sentence must end with one of those.
- Every chapter starts with a heading line: # , then a short German title
  of one to four words that says what the chapter is about, then space,
  em dash (—), space, and its English translation. No chapter number, no
  period at the end. Put an empty line before it:

  # Der erste Tag — The first day

  Only the German part is read aloud ("Der erste Tag."). The player app
  shows each chapter as a section: German title big, English under it, so
  I can jump between chapters and repeat one.
- Make a new chapter every 3 to 6 minutes of audio (about 350 to 650
  words), at a natural change of scene or topic.

THE WORD MUST APPEAR IN THE TEXT

I check coverage by looking for each german entry, minus der/die/das, as
an exact piece of text, ignoring upper and lower case. Endings added to
the end are fine ("Atom" is found in "Atome"). These cases fail, so avoid
them:

- Umlaut plurals: "Glas" is not found in "Gläser".
- Separable verbs: "auflösen" is not found in "löst sich auf". Use the
  infinitive: "kann sich auflösen".
- Participles: "verbrauchen" is not found in "verbraucht".
- Adjective stem changes: "sauer" is not found in "saure"; use "ist
  sauer".
- Multi-word terms with changed endings: "die molare Masse" is not found
  in "der molaren Masse".

LIMITS OF THE VOICE GENERATOR (Supertonic text-to-speech)

The text is read aloud exactly as written. An unknown character makes that
sentence fail. An abbreviation also puts a false pause in the middle of a
sentence. So:

- No digits at all. Write numbers as words: "sieben komma vier",
  "hundertvierzig Millimol pro Liter", "am dritten Mai".
- No abbreviations: "zum Beispiel", not "z. B."; "Frau Doktor Brandt",
  not "Dr. Brandt". No "usw.", "bzw.", "ca.", "Nr." or "d. h.". A period
  followed by a capital letter always ends a sentence.
- No chemical formulas, subscripts or superscripts (not H₂O, not CO₂).
  Write names: "Wasser", "Kohlendioxid".
- No unit symbols (not mg/L, °C, µl or %). Write them out: "Milligramm pro
  Liter", "Grad Celsius", "Mikroliter", "Prozent".
- No arrows, Greek letters, ±, ×, ≈, brackets, slashes or emoji.
- Dialogue: best without quotation marks ("Willkommen, sagt er, ..."). If
  you use them, never use „ ‚ › ‹, which the model rejects.
- Don't write "..." in the middle of a sentence; it ends the sentence
  there.
- Characters tested and accepted: letters, ä ö ü Ä Ö Ü ß, and
  , . : ; - – — … ! ? ' " “ ” ‘ ’ » « € % &

OUTPUT

Plain text only, no code block and no commentary. The first line is the
title, ending with a period. Because the text is long, write it in parts
of about [4] chapters each. After each part I'll say "continue". At the
end, list any vocabulary words you couldn't use, so I can check them.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

AFTER THE CHAT (for you, not for Claude)

1. Paste all parts into one file, e.g. story.txt, saved as UTF-8 (Notepad
   does this by default). Keep the empty lines between paragraphs.
2. Check it:           python -m germanaudio story.txt --text --check
   (It prints the number of sentences and paragraphs.)
3. Preview:            python -m germanaudio story.txt --text --limit 20 -o out/preview.mp3
4. Build it:           python -m germanaudio story.txt --text -o out/story.mp3
   Always give -o its own name, otherwise it overwrites out/german.mp3.
Build the vocabulary track first. Story sentences that match your vocab
phrases are then reused from the cache, which makes the story build faster.
